How crypto builders get targeted

In October 2026, one of our own team was worked by four different lures in two weeks. None of them touched the blockchain. All of them went after the person. Here is what they tried, what stopped them, and the attacker details so others can block them.

Why builders

Thieves rarely break the code. They go after the people who hold keys, run servers, or install tools. The FBI and CISA warned in 2022 that North Korea's TraderTraitor group targets crypto workers with fake job offers and poisoned apps (CISA AA22-108A). The record $6.3 billion Binance theft on October 29, 2026 started with one engineer's laptop (Elliptic).

We have no evidence that the crew in our case was North Korean. Copycat crews use the same playbook. That is the point: the lures are the same at every size.

The four lures

1. Fake support, within minutes (Oct 10)

An email ping from "bn_mod00," then a Discord DM from "@BNB Chain | SUPPORT ✪" two seconds later. Two more fake "BNB Chain Support" accounts followed. Real exchanges never DM you first.

2. A poisoned zip from "a partner" (Oct 17)

A game project sent over Telegram, hosted on GitHub. Inside: an obfuscated script with an encrypted second stage. In our sandbox it crashed before stage two ran, but it was built to run code on the machine.

3. A fake Chrome DevTools site (Oct 19–22)

Pushed by a Telegram bot. The page blocked screenshots and tried to get visitors to paste code into the browser console, scan a QR code, or connect a wallet. Real developer tools never ask you to paste code from a stranger.

4. A "$100 airdrop" QR code (Oct 23)

A casino affiliate page with a countdown ("830 spots remaining") and a WalletConnect QR. Scanning it opened two sessions in MetaMask disguised as "Trust Wallet." Free money that needs a wallet connection is a drainer.

What held: no transaction or signature was approved, no recovery phrase was typed anywhere, and the wallet apps' own storage showed no trace of the drainer site. The sessions were disconnected, cookies wiped, the phone audited, and every lure reported. Nothing was lost.

The rules that worked

  1. Keep wallets on your phone, not in the browser you code and research in.
  2. Never run code someone sends you, including "job tests," game builds and "quick fixes." Open it in a throwaway machine or not at all.
  3. Never paste anything into a browser console because a website told you to.
  4. Read every wallet pop-up. Reject anything you did not start: "Approve," "Permit," "Upgrade to smart account," or any EIP-7702 authorization.
  5. Support never DMs first. Not exchanges, not wallets, not "recovery" services. Go to the official site yourself.
  6. Your recovery phrase is never typed anywhere. Not to "verify," "sync," "rescue" or "claim."
  7. Keep receipts. Screenshots, links and times turn a scare into a report that gets sites taken down.
