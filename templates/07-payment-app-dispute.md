# 7. Payment app dispute (Cash App, Venmo, PayPal, etc.)

In the US, unauthorized electronic transfers from a consumer account are generally covered by the Electronic Fund Transfer Act and Regulation E. That usually means the company must investigate, give you a written result, and, if it denies you, give you the documents it relied on on request.

**Report fast.** How much you can be held responsible for depends on how quickly you report. **Do everything in writing inside the app's support chat** so it's attached to your case.

## A. Dispute message (send for each unauthorized transfer)

> I'm disputing unauthorized electronic fund transfers from my account under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act and Regulation E. I did not make or authorize these transactions:
>
> | Date | Amount | Recipient / description | Transaction ID |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | [DATE] | [$] | [NAME/$cashtag] | [ID] |
>
> I first noticed them on [DATE]. I did not give anyone my login, PIN, or card, and I did not receive anything in return. Please open an investigation, send me the written result, and confirm the case number.

## B. Records request (send in the same chat)

> For each transaction above, please provide and preserve:
> 1. The device, device ID, and app version used
> 2. The IP address and approximate location
> 3. How it was authenticated (PIN, password, biometric, sign-in code)
> 4. Any sign-ins, new devices, or changes to my phone number, email, PIN, or linked accounts within 7 days before
>
> Please also preserve all records related to these transactions while the dispute is open.

## C. If they deny it

> I received your denial on [DATE]. Under Regulation E, I request copies of the documents you relied on to conclude these transfers were authorized. I continue to dispute them because [e.g., the device/location in your records isn't mine; I was at work at that time; my account settings were changed without my knowledge on DATE].

Then attach anything that supports you (proof of where you were, your own device's details) and escalate to a [CFPB complaint](08-cfpb-complaint.md).

*General information, not legal advice. Rules differ for credit cards, business accounts, and outside the US.*
